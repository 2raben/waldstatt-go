# Game idea — Waldstatt Go

Rivals fell their way through the forest round Einsiedeln and win each other's
Waldleute by enclosing them, as in Go. The board is a piece of the real map,
with the rivers and lakes of the [Waldstatt](https://waldstatt-lab.github.io/waldstatt/)
(`waldstatt-lab/waldstatt`), the building game this idea comes from. Everything else is
new: no buildings and no economy, only groups of Waldleute, their axes, and the
forest growing back behind them.

## What it is

Each rival starts with a few Waldleute scattered in an unbroken forest. Groups
cut along lines the player draws; what they cut stays open for a while and then
grows back. Closing a ring of open ground round another rival's group wins it
over, with its people and the trees it has felled. Rivers and lakes are walls,
unless a group crosses them in time. A game ends when its time is up or one
rival is left.

## Why this game

- **Every tree is a move.** Felling, the Waldstatt's core verb, decides the game.
- **Small rules, deep play.** Merging makes a group fast; splitting gives more
  trail to build rings with. Rings must close before the trail grows back.
- **The real map is the board.** The Sihl, the Alp and the lakes make edges and
  corners that change where rings can close.

## Directions it might take

These are examples, not commitments:

- Multiplayer between native apps, on the deterministic simulation.
- Computer rivals written in a small script language players can read and
  replace.
- More boards from the rest of Switzerland, or the Sihlsee filled.
- Phones and touch input in the browser.
